# IAS 1 — Presentation of Financial Statements

Structural and presentation requirements for a complete set of IFRS
financial statements.

## Complete Set of Financial Statements

1. Statement of financial position (as at the end of the period)
2. Statement of profit or loss and other comprehensive income (for the
   period) — either as a single combined statement, or as two statements
   (a separate income statement plus a statement of comprehensive income
   starting from profit/loss)
3. Statement of changes in equity
4. Statement of cash flows
5. Notes, including a summary of significant accounting policies and other
   explanatory information
6. A statement of financial position as at the beginning of the earliest
   comparative period, **only when** a retrospective restatement,
   retrospective application of a new policy, or reclassification is
   applied that has a material effect on that earlier position (i.e., a
   "third balance sheet")

Comparative information is required for the **prior period** at minimum for
all amounts reported, plus comparative narrative/descriptive information
where relevant.

## Statement of Financial Position

Present a **classified** (current/non-current) balance sheet unless a
liquidity-based presentation provides more relevant and reliable
information, in which case all assets/liabilities are presented broadly in
order of liquidity, with disclosure of amounts expected to be settled within
vs. beyond 12 months for items mixing both.

An asset is current if the entity expects to realize it, intends to
sell/consume it, in its normal operating cycle, or expects to realize it
within 12 months, or it's cash/cash-equivalent (unless restricted beyond 12
months). A liability is current if it's expected to be settled in the
normal operating cycle, held primarily for trading, due within 12 months, or
the entity doesn't have an unconditional right to defer settlement for at
least 12 months after the reporting date (note: a covenant breach that
gives the lender the right to demand repayment generally makes the
liability current, even if the lender doesn't intend to call it, **unless**
a waiver is obtained on or before the reporting date — a waiver obtained
after year-end but before the statements are authorized for issue does not
change the classification at the reporting date).

## Profit or Loss and Other Comprehensive Income (OCI)

OCI items are grouped into two categories:
- **Items that will be reclassified ("recycled") to profit or loss** when
  specific conditions are met — e.g., foreign currency translation
  differences, cash flow hedge effective portion, FVOCI debt instrument
  gains/losses.
- **Items that will never be reclassified to profit or loss** — e.g.,
  revaluation surplus on PP&E/intangibles under the revaluation model,
  remeasurements of defined benefit pension plans, FVOCI equity instrument
  gains/losses (if elected under IFRS 9).

Expenses are presented either by **nature** (raw materials, employee
benefits, depreciation, etc.) or by **function** (cost of sales, selling,
administrative) — if by function, additional by-nature disclosure (at
minimum depreciation, amortization, and employee benefits expense) is
required in the notes.

**No "extraordinary items"** — presenting any income/expense item as
"extraordinary" on the face of the statements or in the notes is not
permitted; unusual/infrequent items are instead disclosed separately within
ordinary profit or loss if material to understanding performance.

## Going Concern and Materiality

Management must assess the entity's ability to continue as a going concern
for at least (but not limited to) 12 months from the reporting date.
Material uncertainties that cast significant doubt on that ability must be
disclosed; if management concludes the entity is not a going concern, the
statements are prepared on a different basis and that basis is disclosed.

Materiality assessments apply to both presentation and disclosure —
immaterial items don't need separate presentation even if a specific
standard would otherwise require a line item, and conversely, information is
included if it could reasonably be expected to influence users' decisions,
even where a checklist item wouldn't strictly require it.

## Modeling / Reporting Checklist

- [ ] Decide current/non-current presentation basis and apply it
      consistently to every line, including split disclosure for items with
      mixed maturities
- [ ] Track OCI items separately by "recycles" vs. "never recycles" — this
      matters for how a model handles cumulative translation adjustment,
      hedge reserves, and pension remeasurements when building retained
      earnings/equity roll-forwards
- [ ] If presenting by function, keep a by-nature breakdown (depreciation,
      amortization, employee costs at minimum) available for the notes
- [ ] Confirm covenant compliance at the reporting date before classifying
      debt as non-current — a breach without a pre-year-end waiver forces
      current classification regardless of intent
- [ ] Don't label anything "extraordinary" in outputs meant to mirror IFRS
      presentation
